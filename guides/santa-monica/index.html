● Los Angeles · Westside

Santa Monica, mostly free

The stairs, the market, the beach house — and the few things worth paying for.

Santa Monica has a reputation as the expensive part of the Westside, and the reputation is only half earned.

The best things here cost nothing: a staircase, a beach house, a farmers market that has been running since 1981.

This guide leads with those, then names the handful of rooms that justify the bill.

The live block refreshes every Sunday night.

Free, and better than the paid version

Start with the stairs.

Two public staircases drop into the canyon at Adelaide and 4th.

It is free, it is genuinely brutal, and it is somehow the most social workout in the city — the same people are there every morning and they will nod at you by week two.

Go before it gets hot, park on the street, and do not attempt to look composed.

Free

The beach house nobody realises is public.

The Annenberg Community Beach House is a public beach house with a courtyard, a splash pad and beach courts, and all of that is free to wander whether or not you swim.

The pool is a small pass on top.

Parking fills by eleven on a weekend, so bike or walk in if you can — that is the difference between a good morning and circling for twenty minutes.

Free

Wednesday, not Saturday.

The Arizona Avenue market on a Wednesday is the one LA's kitchens actually shop at.

Sixty-plus farms, a chefs' market, eight in the morning until one.

The Saturday market is bigger and worse.

Worth paying for

Four rooms and a desk.

West Side Oyster Club on Ocean Avenue does lobster rolls and chowder bread bowls looking at the Pacific, and it is counter service, which keeps it honest.

Hermanito on Broadway is where we have run a long-table dinner, which is the strongest thing we can say about a room.

Back on the Beach Café puts breakfast directly on the sand.

And Blankspaces on Colorado is the answer when you have brought a laptop to the beach and made a mistake.
