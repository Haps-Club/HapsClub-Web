Venice, walked west of Lincoln

One day, on foot, in the order that actually works.

Everything below sits west of Lincoln Boulevard — meaning you don't have to leave, and arguably you never should.

This is not a list of the twenty best things in Venice. It is one day, in sequence, built out of places we have actually sent people to in the newsletter.

Morning

Start at the bakery, not the boardwalk.

Gjusta first, and early.

It is a bakery in a warehouse off Sunset Avenue and it is the single best argument for getting to Venice before ten.

Eat there rather than taking it away — the yard is the point.

Late morning

The canals, which almost no local has walked.

Park near Washington and Pacific and walk the bridges.

A mile and a half of water, front gardens, and people who have clearly thought hard about their porch furniture.

It is free, it takes about forty minutes, and it is quietly one of the most romantic miles in the city.

Afternoon

Abbot Kinney, top to bottom.

Walk the boulevard for the shop windows, then drop down to the boardwalk at the skate park and watch for a while.

Back up Rose Avenue for a flat white at Menotti's or pastries at Great White.

The whole loop is about two miles and the entire afternoon costs the price of a coffee.

Dinner

Four rooms, in rough order of how hard they are to get into.

Gjelina is still the one to beat and still takes walk-ins at the bar if you are willing to wait.

The Tasting Kitchen reopened with Casey Lane back in the kitchen, which is the reason to go rather than a nostalgia note.

Felix is the hardest table on the street; if you are only keeping one reservation in Venice, keep that one.

If you only do one thing

Walk the canals at golden hour, then eat at Gjelina's bar without a reservation. That is the whole of Venice in three hours.

Getting there

Park once, then walk.

The mistake is driving between Abbot Kinney, the canals and the boardwalk — they are all within the same twenty-minute walk.

Park near Washington and Pacific for the canals end, or on a residential street east of Abbot Kinney and accept the two-block hike.

Beach lot parking is the expensive way to do it and the lots fill by late morning on weekends.
