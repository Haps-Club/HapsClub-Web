Date ideas in LA, sorted by the kind of date

A first date wants an easy exit. A fifth date wants something to do. Here is which is which.

The trouble with most date-idea lists is that they treat every date the same. A first date and an anniversary want opposite things. The first one wants something short, cheap and easy to extend if it is going well. Later ones want a thing to do, because sitting across a table is the least interesting way to learn about a person.

So this is sorted by the kind of date. Everything here was checked on the venue's own site, and the Westside ones are marked.

First dates

Short, cheap, and easy to extend.

The best first date in LA is a free museum on a weekday afternoon. The Hammer is small enough to see in an hour, needs no reservation, and Westwood has a dozen places to keep talking if it is going well. The Getty on a Saturday stays open until nine, so you can catch the sunset from the garden.

On a Sunday, walk Melrose Trading Post together; a flea market gives you something to talk about every thirty seconds. If you are both readers, an evening author talk at Skylight Books answers the question of what to talk about over the drink afterwards.

Do something

For the second date onwards.

A pottery wheel is the best date activity in the city because both of you will be bad at it. Good Dirt in Del Rey runs two-hour intro classes for six people. Moonlight Rollerway in Glendale still has an organ and a disco ball, and Highland Park Bowl is a 1927 bowling alley with walk-in lanes.

For film, the New Beverly shows only prints, often as a double feature, and Vidiots in Eagle Rock has a bar and a video store you can browse while you wait. The Sunset Ranch evening ride up Mount Hollywood is the one to book for a birthday.

Big nights

Book these.

Largo is the best comedy room in the city: forty dollars, no phones, and you never quite know who will turn up. For music, Sam First near LAX is a small jazz room where people actually listen, and Catalina on Sunset is the dinner-and-a-set version. The Geffen in Westwood is the theater night, and the Magic Castle is the one to save for when a member owes you a favour.

Free

The good ones cost nothing.

Griffith Observatory after dark is free, open until ten, and the lawn looks straight down at the city. Downtown, take the free audio tour of Disney Hall and walk up to the rooftop garden, then cross the street to The Broad, which is free too. On the Westside, the Venice canals at dusk are a free forty-minute walk.

Rainy days

This is an El Niño winter.

When it rains, go indoors and stay a while. The Academy Museum drops to $18 after 4:30pm, which is the right time to go anyway. The Last Bookstore is open until eleven most nights. Every film, bowling and comedy pick above works in the rain too.

If you only do one thing

The Hammer on a weekday afternoon, then a walk through Westwood. Free, an hour if it is not working, the whole evening if it is.

Quick answers

Common questions

What is a good first date in Los Angeles?

Something short, cheap and easy to extend: the Hammer Museum in Westwood is free, needs no reservation and takes about an hour, and Westwood has plenty of places to keep talking afterwards.

What are free date ideas in LA?

Griffith Observatory at night (free, open until 10pm Tuesday to Sunday), The Broad and the Hammer (both free), the free self-guided tour of Walt Disney Concert Hall, and the Getty, which is free with a timed reservation.

What are romantic things to do in LA at night?

Griffith Observatory's lawn over the city lights, the Getty on a Saturday when it stays open until 9pm, a sunset trail ride at Sunset Ranch Hollywood, or a jazz set at Sam First or Catalina Jazz Club.

What are good rainy day date ideas in Los Angeles?

A double feature on film at the New Beverly, Vidiots in Eagle Rock, bowling at Highland Park Bowl, the Academy Museum after 4:30pm for $18, or The Last Bookstore, open until 11pm Wednesday to Saturday.

Where can you see comedy on a date in LA?

Largo at the Coronet on La Cienega: most shows are $40, no phones, doors at 7pm and the show at 8pm.
