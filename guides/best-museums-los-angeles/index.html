Which museum, for which mood

Eleven that are worth the drive, sorted by what you actually feel like doing.

LA has no single museum you have to see, which is the problem. There are a dozen good ones, scattered across forty miles, and the wrong choice means a bad parking garage and a room you did not want.

So this is not a ranking. It is a way to pick: how much time you have, whether you want contemporary or old, indoors or out. Each one is linked to its own site, and the free ones say so.

Big art

Five that fill an afternoon.

The Getty Center is the one for a first visit: a hilltop of Richard Meier architecture and gardens that explains the city before you look at a single painting. Free, but you need a timed reservation and the parking is $25.

LACMA is the only one of the five that charges, and a campus you can lose a day in. If you are a county resident, go on a weekday after 3pm and it is free; everyone else gets the second Tuesday of the month.

The Broad and MOCA sit a block apart on Grand Avenue, both free, both contemporary, and you can do them in one trip. The Broad's Infinity Mirrored Room needs its own advance reservation. The Hammer in Westwood is the small, easy one: free, no reservation, two hours is plenty.

Film, dinosaurs and space

For when you want a reason to go, not a gallery.

The Academy Museum is the film one and the only one in this group that closes on Tuesdays. The Natural History Museum is the dinosaurs, and with the free county-resident hours on weekday afternoons it is the cheapest good afternoon in the city.

The California Science Center is free to walk in; budget separately for IMAX and the special exhibitions.

Gardens and quiet rooms

Slower, and further out.

The Huntington is the one to spend a whole day on: two hundred acres, and the Japanese and Chinese gardens are the reason to go. Buy timed tickets and skip Tuesdays.

Norton Simon in Pasadena is a compact, serious collection with a free parking lot. Skirball on the Sepulveda Pass is free on Thursdays, with free parking, which makes it the best-value weekday visit on the Westside.

Plan around the closed day

Almost every one closes on a different day.

The mistake that wastes a drive is turning up on the day that museum is dark. Here is the day each one is closed.

If you only do one thing

The Getty on a weekday morning, then lunch in Brentwood. It is free, it is on the Westside, and it works for someone who does not like museums.

What we could not verify

Left off on purpose.

The Museum of Jurassic Technology on Venice Boulevard is a real favourite, but its hours and admission are not on any page we could read, so we are not printing them. The Petersen Automotive Museum lists different prices on different pages of its own site.
